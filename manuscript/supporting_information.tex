\section{Scope and Data Provenance}
This Supplementary Material documents the calibrated fingerprint transfer and sensitivity analyses reported in the main text. The experimental response channel and frequency axis are archived as \path{newest_data/heat2_fud.txt} and \path{newest_data/xaxis_fud.txt}, respectively. They were supplied by J.~D.~Cyran and are associated with the silica/water experiments described in Ref.~\cite{Cyran2019PNAS}. They are numerical source data, rather than a digitization of the published experimental figure. The archive contains one channel, so the present analysis does not independently fit a full complex real/imaginary susceptibility pair.

\section{Experimental Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Sample Preparation}
Infrasil 302 silica windows, \SI{25}{mm} in diameter and \SI{2}{mm} thick (Korth Kristalle), were used as substrates. The windows were sonicated in ethanol and water, then immersed in 3:1 piranha solution for \SI{10}{min}. The windows were heated at \SI{950}{\degreeCelsius} for \SI{4}{h}, allowed to cool, and immersed in 3:1 acidic piranha solution for \SI{10}{min}. Glassware was sonicated in Deconex, ethanol, and Millipore water for \SI{15}{min}, then rinsed thoroughly with Millipore water.

\subsection{Phase-Resolved SFG Spectroscopy}
The spectrometer uses a Ti:sapphire regenerative amplifier (Spitfire Ace, Spectra-Physics) producing approximately \SI{40}{fs} pulses centered at \SI{800}{nm}. One portion of the output drives an optical parametric amplifier (TOPAS-Prime, Light Conversion) with a noncollinear difference-frequency-generation stage, producing IR pulses centered near \SI{3600}{cm^{-1}}. A second portion, approximately \SI{1}{mJ}, passes through an etalon to generate a narrowband visible pulse with a bandwidth of \SI{25}{cm^{-1}}, which determines the spectral resolution.

The IR and visible beams are focused with lenses of \SI{5}{cm} and \SI{100}{cm} focal length, respectively, and overlap spatially and temporally at a gold mirror to produce a local oscillator (LO). A curved mirror with a \SI{5}{cm} focal length collects the IR, visible, and LO beams and focuses them onto an Infrasil 302 window in contact with water. At the silica/air boundary, the IR and visible incidence angles are approximately \ang{45} and \ang{40} from the surface normal. A \SI{1}{mm} fused-silica plate introduces a delay between the LO and the IR/visible pulses. The LO and sample SFG signal are sent to a spectrometer (Acton SP-2300, Princeton Instruments) and detected with an EMCCD camera (Newton 970, Andor). Spectra were acquired for \SI{5}{min} in the $ssp$ polarization combination, denoting $s$-polarized SFG, $s$-polarized visible, and $p$-polarized IR. A gold-coated silica window provides the reference.

\section{Experimental Trace and Window Diagnostics}
The source files contain 1600 paired frequency and response values. There are 1578 finite response values and 22 nonfinite values between \SI{2674.4}{cm^{-1}} and \SI{2883.5}{cm^{-1}}. All original response values at frequencies of \SI{3000}{cm^{-1}} and above are finite. For plotting and smoothing the full trace, nonfinite values are linearly interpolated from adjacent finite measurements; no fitted point in the reported windows is replaced by interpolation.

A moving average is applied to the full trace before window selection. For odd width $q$, the arithmetic mean of the $q$ neighboring samples is used, with repeated endpoint values for padding. The primary width is $q=17$; $q=1$ denotes the unsmoothed trace. Widths 7, 11, 25, and 33 are also tested. Averaging is performed on the supplied point grid, not on an assumed uniform frequency grid.

\section{Fingerprint Extraction and Frequency Calibration}
The molecular dictionary is taken from the total imaginary \sfg panels of the reference \waterair calculation.\cite{Kaliannan2026Rosetta} Its eight motifs are extracted from the magenta Total curves in \path{SFG_A.eps}, \path{SFG_B.eps}, and \path{SFG_L2.eps}. The code reads the vector paths, discards the repeated filled-polygon path and its artificial closing segments, and averages duplicate frequency coordinates. It uses the explicit horizontal zero line in each panel; no baseline is inferred from the curve tails.

In the Total column of each source EPS file, the labeled \SI{3000}{cm^{-1}} and \SI{3800}{cm^{-1}} ticks have horizontal coordinates 3057 and 4110. A path coordinate $X$ is converted using
\begin{equation}
 \nu(X)=3000+\frac{800(X-3057)}{4110-3057}\quad\si{cm^{-1}}.
 \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
The paths themselves extend approximately from \SI{2899.7}{cm^{-1}} to \SI{3900.3}{cm^{-1}}. Mapping their endpoints to \SI{3000}{cm^{-1}} and \SI{3800}{cm^{-1}} would compress the frequency scale by about 20\%, changing both band positions and widths. The corrected motif-I maximum is near \SI{3660.2}{cm^{-1}}. Finite coordinate precision in the source EPS files limits the precision of the extracted curves, and access to the original numerical simulation spectra would permit a more accurate transfer.

\section{Constrained Spectral Fit}
For a selected window and common shift $\Delta$, each fingerprint is linearly interpolated onto the experimental grid and RMS normalized:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat f_j(\nu_i;\Delta)=\frac{f_j(\nu_i-\Delta)}{\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_j(\nu_i-\Delta)^2}}.
\end{equation}
Interpolation outside the source support returns zero, although the reported windows and \SI{\pm20}{cm^{-1}} shift range remain within that support. The fitted model is
\begin{equation}
 y_{\rm fit}(\nu_i)=b_0+b_1\frac{\nu_i-\overline\nu}{\nu_{\max}-\nu_{\min}}
 +\sigma\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}w_j\widehat f_j(\nu_i;\Delta),\qquad w_j\geq0.
\end{equation}
The two background coefficients are unconstrained. The sign $\sigma=\pm1$ is tested globally, and $\Delta$ is scanned in \SI{1}{cm^{-1}} steps between $-20$ and $+20$~\si{cm^{-1}}. Each model and control undergoes the same optimization. The reported solution has the smallest residual sum of squares (SSE).

The solver projects out the two background columns and examines every active subset of the allowed motifs, including the empty subset. Negative least-squares coefficients are rejected for that subset; zero coefficients and lower-dimensional boundaries remain feasible. The final solution is checked against the nonnegative least-squares optimality conditions. Allowing an additional motif can therefore preserve or decrease SSE, but cannot worsen it. This check detects the earlier error of retaining only strictly positive interior solutions.

The descriptive coefficient of determination is $R^2=1-\mathrm{SSE}/\sum_i(y_i-\overline y)^2$. The tabulated spectral fraction is $w_j/\sum_k w_k$ and depends on RMS normalization over the selected window. It is not a molecular population. Conventional F-test probabilities and information-criterion rankings are not used because smoothed points are correlated, shifts and motif sets are optimized, and coefficients can lie on constraint boundaries. The data archive provides neither replicate-based uncertainty nor a noise covariance model.

\section{Structural Scope and Reproducibility}
The schematic groups L1--L3 are distinct from the reference instantaneous-layer labels: L1 and L2 of the silica/water schematic correspond to regions A and B of the reference topmost layer, and schematic L3 corresponds to its second layer. No metric distances or individual hydrogen bonds are inferred from the fit. VII and VIII have different polar orientations and partially opposing fingerprints; they are not merely azimuthal mirror images. The I--VI--VIII geometry in main-text Fig.~4 is an illustrative hypothesis, since VI has zero coefficient in the calibrated primary-window fit.
