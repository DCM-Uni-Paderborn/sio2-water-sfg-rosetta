\begin{abstract}
Phase-resolved vibrational sum-frequency generation (SFG) reports on interfacial water with orientational sensitivity, but converting an OH-stretch spectrum into a molecular structure remains an underdetermined inverse problem. We revisit the buried silica/water spectrum of Cyran \textit{et al.} by using species-resolved water--air SFG fingerprints as a structural Rosetta stone. The transfer is made in the experimental viewing convention, where positive \imchi corresponds to OH/H orientation toward the silica window. To avoid converting a phenomenological line-shape reproduction into a population model, we assign structure only in the \SIrange{3400}{3800}{cm^{-1}} water-fingerprint window. There, a nonnegative three-motif layered model using motifs I, II, and VIII reproduces the measured imaginary response with $R^2=0.972$ and $p=5.2\times10^{-155}$ relative to a linear-background model. Since related reduced fits are possible, the assignment is not presented as a unique algebraic decomposition. Rather, I+II+VIII is the minimal three-motif model that simultaneously preserves all populated water strata, gives the best clean-window fit, and supports a continuous hydrogen-bond topology. The high-frequency weak-OH feature at silica/water is therefore not an isolated dangling population; it is the spectroscopic end of a hydrogen-bonded interfacial water network.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Vibrational \sfg spectroscopy is one of the few techniques that can directly access the orientation of water at buried interfaces, but its structural interpretation is rarely local or additive.\cite{Shen2006ChemRev,Bonn2015Angew,Tang2020ChemRev} The measured second-order susceptibility is a coherent response, shaped by orientational selection rules, OH-stretch frequencies, hydrogen-bond topology, vibrational coupling, and phase convention. This makes the inverse problem especially delicate at mineral/water interfaces, where the substrate can impose electrostatic order while also contributing nonresonant and low-frequency optical response.

The amorphous silica/water interface is a particularly useful test case. Cyran and co-workers measured a phase-resolved \sfg spectrum through the silica side and observed positive imaginary OH-stretch features near \SI{3400}{cm^{-1}} and \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}}.\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} In that experimental convention, positive \imchi corresponds to hydrogen atoms pointing toward the negatively charged silica surface. The high-frequency feature was assigned to weakly hydrogen-bonded, quasi-free OH groups pointing toward siloxane bridges, revealing molecular hydrophobicity at a surface that is hydrophilic on macroscopic length scales. What remains less explicit is the water structure that supports this response. Is the \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}} feature an isolated dangling-OH population, or is it a marker embedded in a broader hydrogen-bond network?

Here we address that question by transferring a motif-resolved \waterair SFG dictionary to the buried silica/water geometry. The purpose is not to replace the original silica/water interpretation, but to sharpen it: the \waterair interface provides a set of water structures for which both molecular orientation and total \sfg fingerprint are known. When this dictionary is used with the correct sign convention and with conservative spectral windows, it becomes a practical Rosetta stone for constructing a water-only layer model at the silica/water interface.

\section{Motif Dictionary and Transfer Geometry}

The reference \waterair analysis resolves the outermost water into structural motifs with distinct orientational distributions and computed total \imchi fingerprints.\cite{Kaliannan2026Rosetta} That model was generated from partially adiabatic centroid molecular dynamics trajectories of the water--air interface: interfacial molecules were assigned to instantaneous layers using the identification of truly interfacial molecules algorithm, sorted into orientational motifs in the local interfacial frame, and then evaluated with the surface-specific velocity--velocity correlation-function formalism to obtain motif-resolved total \sfg responses.\cite{Ohto2015JCP,Kaliannan2020MolPhys} The dilute top layer contains motif I, in which one OH is weakly hydrogen bonded or nearly free while the other remains connected to the liquid. The denser interfacial layer contains bridge and in-plane motifs II--VI. A second water layer contains motifs VII and VIII, whose bonded-OH responses are partially opposed and can cancel. Figure~\ref{fig:rosetta-pairs} displays the full one-to-one dictionary used here: each motif is paired directly with its total \sfg fingerprint from the reference paper.

For the buried-interface model, we relabel the selected depth sequence simply as L1--L3. These labels define a three-motif layered structural model rather than metric slabs: the layer order is inherited from the instantaneous interfacial definition of the \waterair analysis and is not converted into arbitrary fixed-thickness slices. The transfer also requires a sign convention. Because the silica/water spectrum was measured through the silica side, the paper-up direction of the \waterair structures is mapped toward silica, and positive \imchi is interpreted as OH/H orientation toward the \ce{SiO2} side. Figure~\ref{fig:rosetta} shows the three motifs ultimately selected by the clean-window structural fit in this transferred geometry.

\section{Spectral Reproduction and Structural Inversion}

We first separate spectral reproducibility from structural assignment. A flexible complex resonance model, including low-edge nuisance terms, reproduces the full nominal OH-stretch region from \SIrange{3300}{3800}{cm^{-1}} with $R^2_\mathrm{complex}=0.990$ and $R^2_\mathrm{Im}=0.983$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fit}a). This confirms that the measured line shape is quantitatively reproducible, but it is not a population analysis. In particular, the lower edge of the window includes response that cannot be uniquely assigned to transferred water motifs and should not be over-interpreted as a clean water-structure reporter.

The structural fit is therefore performed against a moving-average-smoothed version of the digitized experimental \imchi curve, not against the phenomenological resonance fit, and is restricted to the \SIrange{3400}{3800}{cm^{-1}} water-fingerprint window. In this interval, a minimal three-motif layered model containing L1/motif I, L2/motif II, and L3/motif VIII reproduces the imaginary spectrum with $R^2=0.972$ and $p=5.2\times10^{-155}$ relative to a linear-background model (Fig.~\ref{fig:fit}b,c). The best global frequency shift is \SI{-25}{cm^{-1}}, a modest displacement for transferring a \waterair fingerprint to a buried mineral/water environment.

The decomposition is structurally constrained but not mathematically unique. A reduced I+VIII scaffold already gives $R^2=0.970$, but leaves the bridge layer empty. An unconstrained I+II fit gives $R^2=0.971$, but leaves the deeper hydrogen-bonded layer absent. Adding motif II to the I+VIII scaffold improves the residual by $\Delta R^2=0.0016$; an approximate nested comparison gives $p=6.5\times10^{-7}$ for this additional structural weight. Thus the evidence for I+II+VIII is cumulative: it is the best clean-window three-motif layered fit, it retains a hydrogen-bond path across L1--L3, and it avoids assigning the lower edge of the spectrum to a water motif without sufficient spectral support. The Supporting Information gives the complete window scan, forced-bridge controls, and alternative weights.

\section{Layered Water Model}

The resulting three-motif water-only model is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:model}. L1 is motif-I-like: one OH points toward silica and gives the weak/free-OH marker, while the partner OH remains connected to the liquid. L2 is populated by a motif-II-like bridge that donates a hydrogen bond to the L1 oxygen, linking the weak-OH side to the interfacial hydrogen-bond sheet. L3 is motif-VIII-like and donates a hydrogen bond to L2. The clean-window RMS fingerprint fractions are 0.375 (I), 0.395 (II), and 0.230 (VIII). These values should be read as spectral weights, not molecular populations, because \sfg intensities depend on orientation, vibrational coupling, and coherent interference.

Only the three selected motifs are shown: L1/motif I supplies the silica-directed weak/free-OH marker, L2/motif II donates a hydrogen bond to the oxygen of L1, and L3/motif VIII donates a hydrogen bond to the oxygen of L2.}

This model refines the molecular-hydrophobicity picture of silica/water. The \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}} response remains a silica-directed weakly hydrogen-bonded OH feature, consistent with the original assignment.\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} The additional point is that this high-frequency marker is not structurally isolated. It is the top spectroscopic signature of a connected water network in which a weakly bonded silica-facing OH is backed by bridge water and deeper hydrogen-bonded water. The interface is therefore locally hydrophobic in its exposed OH response while remaining hydrogen-bond continuous underneath.

The lower-frequency side of the spectrum remains important but less structurally specific in the present transfer. When the entire \SIrange{3300}{3800}{cm^{-1}} region is forced to be described only by paper fingerprints, the best layer-populated model contains I, VI, VII, and VIII but reaches only $R^2\approx0.64$. We therefore do not assign the sub-\SI{3400}{cm^{-1}} response uniquely to water orientation. The appearance of motif VI in edge-sensitive controls is nevertheless chemically consistent with a bridge-family L2 layer: motif II is selected by the clean high-frequency fingerprint, whereas VI may represent more deeply connected bridge response when collective lower-frequency contributions are included.

\section{Conclusions}

The water--air Rosetta stone converts the phase-resolved silica/water spectrum into a compact molecular picture: a three-motif hydrogen-bonded water network in which a motif-I-like L1 water supplies the silica-directed weak/free-OH marker, a motif-II-like L2 water bridges to the first layer, and a motif-VIII-like L3 water completes the deeper hydrogen-bond connection. This model preserves the central conclusion of Cyran \textit{et al.} that the \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}} feature reports molecularly hydrophobic silica-facing OH groups, but it changes the structural emphasis. The high-frequency response is not treated as an isolated dangling species; it is the terminal spectroscopic fingerprint of a connected interfacial water network.

More generally, the analysis illustrates how structure-resolved \sfg fingerprints can be used as a transferable spectroscopic dictionary. Once the viewing geometry, sign convention, and reliable water-fingerprint window are defined, experimentally measured \imchi spectra can be interpreted by nonnegative combinations of molecularly assigned total-\sfg responses rather than by anonymous resonances alone. In this sense, the water--air Rosetta stone provides a practical route for assigning water structures at aqueous interfaces, including buried solid/water interfaces, from phase-resolved \sfg spectra with high quantitative fidelity.

\section{Methods}

The experimental silica/water spectrum was digitized from the phase-resolved \sfg data of Cyran \textit{et al.}\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} The digitized complex spectrum was smoothed by a 17-point moving average for plotting and least-squares stability. Full-window spectral reproduction used a complex Lorentzian line-shape model with resonant terms and low-frequency nuisance terms. Structural fits used species-resolved total \imchi fingerprints digitized from the \waterair motif analysis.\cite{Kaliannan2026Rosetta} Each fingerprint was shifted over a discrete frequency grid and combined by nonnegative least squares with a floating linear baseline. Structural fits were performed against the moving-average-smoothed digitized experimental imaginary spectrum, not against the phenomenological resonance fit. Reported $p$ values are F-test probabilities for residual reduction relative to a linear-background model. All structural weights are RMS-normalized fingerprint contributions.
