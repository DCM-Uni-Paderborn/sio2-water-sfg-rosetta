\begin{abstract}
Phase-resolved vibrational sum-frequency generation (SFG) probes interfacial water orientation, but its conversion into molecular structure remains an underdetermined inverse problem. We test a molecularly resolved water--air SFG dictionary against an experimental silica/water response trace. Eight orientational motifs provide signed spectral fingerprints, transferred to the silica-side viewing convention and combined with nonnegative coefficients and a linear background. Frequency calibration against the source axes is essential. Over \SIrange{3300}{3800}{cm^{-1}}, an I+VIII representation gives $R^2=0.9918$, while an oppositely oriented VII+VIII combination gives $R^2=0.9920$. Restricting the analysis to \SIrange{3400}{3800}{cm^{-1}} favors I+VIII and gives $R^2=0.9980$. The high-frequency response is therefore compatible with the weakly hydrogen-bonded OH motif identified in earlier silica/water experiments, but its fitted contribution depends on the analysis window. A proposed motif-VI connector is not required in the broader window. The dictionary provides a transparent test of molecular interpretations while exposing which assignments remain unresolved by a single coherent spectrum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Vibrational \sfg spectroscopy accesses water at buried interfaces with sensitivity to molecular orientation.\cite{Shen2006ChemRev,Bakker2016ChemRev,Bonn2015Angew,Tang2020ChemRev} Its structural interpretation is complicated by the coherent nature of the response: OH-stretch frequencies, orientational selection, hydrogen bonding, and vibrational coupling jointly determine the observed line shape. At charged mineral/water interfaces, screening and interference can also change the depth sampled by \sfg.\cite{Schaefer2017PCCP} A molecular assignment must therefore address both spectral agreement and the alternatives that the data permit.

Silica/water is a particularly instructive example. Cyran and co-workers observed positive imaginary OH-stretch features in silica-side phase-resolved \sfg, including a weakly hydrogen-bonded OH feature near \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}}.\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} Phase and isotopic measurements supported an assignment to water OH groups directed toward siloxane environments. This local molecular hydrophobicity coexists with macroscopic hydrophilicity. The water--air interface offers a useful reference because its weak/free-OH groups remain connected to a fluctuating hydrogen-bond network.\cite{Kuhne2011JPCL,Kessler2015JPCB} Experiments and simulations have connected interfacial-water heterogeneity, dynamics, and orientational order to the \sfg response.\cite{Hsieh2014Angew,Cyran2018JPCB,Sulpizi2013JPCL,Ohto2019JPCL,Kaliannan2020MolPhys}

Here we use a motif-resolved water--air dictionary as a Rosetta stone for the silica/water spectrum. Each basis function has a molecular interpretation and includes the calculated total \sfg response of its motif. The transfer is a testable approximation: the substrate can change frequencies, orientational distributions, and coupling, so agreement with a transferred fingerprint does not establish identical microscopic environments. We examine how frequency calibration, analysis window, smoothing, and alternative motif combinations affect the assignment.

\section{Molecular Dictionary and Viewing Convention}

The reference dictionary was obtained from partially adiabatic centroid molecular dynamics at the water--air interface.\cite{Kaliannan2026Rosetta} Instantaneous interfacial layers were identified with the identification of truly interfacial molecules algorithm, and molecules were grouped by their orientation relative to the surface normal. Motif-resolved spectra were calculated using the surface-specific velocity--velocity correlation-function formalism.\cite{Ohto2015JCP,Kaliannan2020MolPhys} Motif I contains one weakly bonded or nearly free OH group. Motifs II--VI describe the denser part of the topmost interfacial layer, and motifs VII and VIII represent oppositely oriented molecules in the second layer. Their total fingerprints contain both local and intermolecular contributions and are consequently not spectra of independent, uncoupled species.

For the silica/water schematic, we label the weak-OH, bridge-family, and second-layer motif groups L1, L2, and L3. The first two groups correspond to regions A and B of the same topmost instantaneous layer in the reference calculation. These schematic labels specify motif groups, not independently measured depths or fixed-thickness slabs. We map the reference normal, directed from water toward vapor, onto the water-to-silica direction. Under the experimental silica-side phase convention, positive \imchi is associated with a net OH orientation toward silica.\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} The global sign tested during fitting compares the digitized dictionary with this convention; it does not determine individual OH orientations independently of the motif calculation.

\section{Spectral Analysis}

We analyze the single response channel supplied with its experimental frequency axis, associated with the silica/water measurements of Cyran \textit{et al.}\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} The archive contains 1600 points, including 22 nonfinite values below \SI{2900}{cm^{-1}}. All points in the fitted OH-stretch windows are finite. Raw-minus-smoothed fluctuations are larger relative to the local amplitude in \SIrange{3200}{3300}{cm^{-1}} than in \SIrange{3300}{3800}{cm^{-1}}. We use the latter as the primary comparison window and retain \SIrange{3200}{3800}{cm^{-1}}, \SIrange{3400}{3800}{cm^{-1}}, and \SIrange{3350}{3700}{cm^{-1}} as controls. These intervals are analysis choices whose consequences are assessed explicitly, rather than independently established boundaries of the interfacial response.

The fitted signal is
\begin{equation}
 y_{\mathrm{fit}}(\nu_i)=b_0+b_1t_i+\sigma\sum_j w_j\widehat f_j(\nu_i;\Delta),\qquad w_j\geq0,
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $t_i$ is a centered, dimensionless frequency coordinate, $b_0$ and $b_1$ are unconstrained background coefficients, and each shifted fingerprint $\widehat f_j$ has unit RMS over the fit window. We test $\sigma=\pm1$ and scan a common shift $\Delta$ from $-20$ to $+20$~\si{cm^{-1}} in \SI{1}{cm^{-1}} steps. For each sign and shift, all coefficient boundaries are included in the nonnegative least-squares solution. The primary trace is smoothed with a 17-point moving average; unsmoothed data and widths of 7, 11, 25, and 33 points provide controls. The Supplementary Material gives the complete procedure and numerical comparisons.

(b) The I+VI+VIII candidate reduces to I+VIII, with $R^2=0.9918$ and $\Delta=-5$~\si{cm^{-1}}; the I+VI+VII+VIII control reduces to VII+VIII, with $R^2=0.9920$ and $\Delta=-8$~\si{cm^{-1}}. Frequency calibration materially affects the interpretation. The EPS curves extend approximately from \SI{2900}{cm^{-1}} to \SI{3900}{cm^{-1}}, beyond the labeled \SI{3000}{cm^{-1}} and \SI{3800}{cm^{-1}} ticks. Calibrating the curve endpoints to those ticks would compress the frequency axis and move the motif-I maximum from about \SI{3660}{cm^{-1}} to \SI{3610}{cm^{-1}}. We instead use the labeled ticks and explicit horizontal zero lines. With this calibration, the I+VI+VIII candidate gives $R^2=0.9918$ and a residual sum of squares of 0.03377, but its VI weight is zero. Adding VII gives a slightly better fit, $R^2=0.9920$, and replaces the small I contribution with a VII+VIII combination. A scan of all one-, two-, and three-motif subsets confirms this alternative within the stated shift range.

Removing VIII and allowing I+VI+VII yields $R^2=0.9545$ in the primary window, with only VI active and the shift at the lower search boundary. Thus VIII supplies the most useful bonded-water fingerprint within the tested dictionary, but the spectrum does not require the proposed three-motif network. The RMS coefficient fractions of I+VIII are 0.0052 and 0.9948. Their values depend on basis normalization, viewing convention, and window choice; they cannot be interpreted as fractions of interfacial water molecules.

The higher-frequency control retains a clearer weak-OH contribution. Over \SIrange{3400}{3800}{cm^{-1}}, I+VIII gives $R^2=0.9980$ with $\Delta=-3$~\si{cm^{-1}}. Adding VI changes $R^2$ by only $3.6\times10^{-6}$. In contrast, widening the interval to \SIrange{3200}{3800}{cm^{-1}} changes the active motifs and lowers agreement. Smoothing preserves good descriptive fits, but does not remove this window dependence. Consequently, the high-frequency response is compatible with motif I and with the original weakly hydrogen-bonded OH assignment,\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} while a unique connector or layer population cannot be extracted from this trace.

The reported $R^2$ values describe agreement with the analyzed line shape. We do not assign conventional F-test probabilities to these optimized fits: smoothing correlates neighboring samples, coefficients can lie on nonnegative boundaries, and motif sets and frequency shifts are selected from the data. No replicate-based uncertainty or noise covariance is supplied. Model comparisons therefore quantify descriptive spectral differences rather than the statistical certainty of a molecular structure.

\section{Structural Interpretation and Limits}

Figure~\ref{fig:model} illustrates how weak-OH, bridge-family, and deeper bonded-water motifs could be connected at silica/water. The arrangement preserves the silica-side viewing convention and the connectivity proposed in the original three-motif picture. After frequency calibration, however, the VI connector is not required by the primary fit. The diagram is therefore a structural hypothesis, whose individual hydrogen bonds and layer spacings are not determined by the one-dimensional spectrum.

L1--L3 denote schematic motif groups, with no metric depth assignment. Dashed lines indicate possible hydrogen bonds; their connectivity is not independently established by the fit. Colored wave packets depict the incident IR and visible pulses and outgoing SFG signal in the silica-side geometry. Their wavelengths, envelopes, and beam angles are schematic. The calibrated primary-window fit gives zero weight to VI.}
  \label{fig:model}

The transferred fingerprints include coupling in the reference water--air environment. Reweighting them at silica/water assumes that those line shapes remain useful despite substrate-induced changes. A fitted linear background may also absorb broad contributions from the substrate or field-oriented water. The analysis does not separately resolve intrinsic $\chi^{(2)}$, field-induced $\chi^{(3)}$, or frequency-dependent optical factors.\cite{Schaefer2017PCCP} These limitations prevent the fitted coefficients from establishing absolute populations or a measured depth profile. A silica-specific forward calculation or additional polarization, phase, or isotopic data would test the competing assignments more directly.

\section{Conclusions}

A calibrated, motif-resolved water--air dictionary gives a molecularly interpretable representation of the silica/water response. The dominant fitted bonded-water contribution is VIII-like, while the weak-OH contribution and admixture of the oppositely oriented VII motif depend on the analysis window. The high-frequency control is consistent with the weakly hydrogen-bonded OH assignment of Cyran \textit{et al.},\cite{Cyran2019PNAS} but the primary window does not require a VI connector or establish a continuous three-layer network. The transferable result is a practical way to compare molecular hypotheses and expose the structural information that a coherent interfacial spectrum does, and does not, resolve.
